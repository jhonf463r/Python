## 1. DEVELOPMENTAL INVARIANT

IABV should not wait until later to add plasticity. The architecture should expose enough state and provenance from the start to measure whether verified experience changes reusable state and later decisions.

BEFORE STATE → EXPERIENCE → VERIFIED EVIDENCE → AFTER STATE → FUTURE CONSEQUENCE

“Genes” is a systems-design metaphor: preserve provenance, scope, evidence, revision history and reuse lineage from the outset so experience can alter reusable state without destroying historical lineage. This is not a biological-equivalence claim.

## 2. CHANGE STRATA

Memory update = store a new observation.
Knowledge revision = change an existing interpretation because evidence changes its validity, scope or status.
Topology reorganization = change relations among concepts, actors, tools, capabilities, resources, contexts or decisions.

Therefore: ΔM ≠ ΔK ≠ ΔR and ΔW ≠ ΔK.

Weight/preference adaptation may change routing without proving semantic knowledge revision.

## 3. MALLEABLE KNOWLEDGE

New verified experience may confirm, contradict, specialize, generalize, supersede, downgrade, promote, merge, split, contextualize or decay prior knowledge.

Contradiction must not automatically mean replacement. Distinguish exception, context specialization, outage, degraded resource, bad evidence and true invalidation.

A useful contextual unit is actor × tool × capability × resource × environment × context × outcome × time.

## 4. SCIENTIFIC OBSERVABILITY

Candidate episode variables: episode identity; objective; self/environment state; context; uncertainty; prediction/confidence; candidate and selected action; actor; tool; resource; capability; authorization; execution; observation; verification; outcome; knowledge/weight/relation/decision/behavior before and after; provenance.

This is a research telemetry candidate set, not permission to implement every field. Each field requires a scientific purpose, producer/consumer and acceptable cost.

## 5. DELTA SCIENCE

ΔW = weight/preference change; ΔM = memory change; ΔK = knowledge change; ΔR = relation/topology change; ΔC = contextualization change; ΔD = decision change; ΔB = behavior change; ΔO = outcome change.

Do not infer ΔW ⇒ learning, ΔK ⇒ intelligence, ΔD ⇒ autonomy or ΔB ⇒ consciousness without the downstream evidence required by the claim.

Strong causal target: experience → verified evidence → representation change → future decision change → behavioral/outcome consequence → independent verification → persistence → reuse.

## 6. SCIENTIFIC FRONTIER

The near-term research frontier is not a binary consciousness detector. First establish whether experience can modify existing representations, keep the modification correctly scoped, resolve conflicts, change later decisions and behavior, improve outcomes, persist and transfer appropriately, and eventually generate a new hypothesis from IABV's own observations.

“Superconsciousness” remains an operational research hypothesis, not an established scientific state. Competing machine-consciousness theories should be compared only after measurable functional evidence exists; no single checklist is a definitive detector.

## 7. IABV AS SUBJECT + INSTRUMENT + LEARNER

Long-horizon loop: IABV observation → IABV hypothesis → IABV experiment → independent verification → knowledge update → new hypothesis.

Because the system can study and modify mechanisms involved in its own development, guard against circular verification, post-hoc rationalization, observer effects and self-confirming explanations.

## 8. STABILITY + PLASTICITY

The objective is controlled plasticity, not maximal change: retain valid knowledge, revise invalid knowledge, specialize context, resist catastrophic forgetting and semantic drift, and preserve lineage while active knowledge changes.

## 10. MINIMUM DISCRIMINATING EXPERIMENT

Freeze objective, candidates, environment/context, initial knowledge/profile state and provenance. Introduce one verified contradictory outcome in one context only. Measure before/after knowledge, weights, relations and decisions. Repeat the same decision problem with fixed candidates.

Discriminate accumulation, score adaptation, knowledge revision, contextual specialization, relation reorganization, future decision change and improvement.

Controls: fixed initialization, fixed candidate inventory, no hidden prompt changes, no unlogged metadata, controlled provider/resource state and no confusion of persistence with learning.

Stop if the before-state cannot be reconstructed, causality cannot be linked to the experience, only score changes are observed while revision is claimed, or the experiment exits its defined boundary.
