## 1. C15-D1-1a/b/c — Ordered and complete acknowledgement-status classification

### 1.1 Shared timebase and status-field scope

For a trusted-ack operation, `expected_delivery_ack_status` reports acknowledgement evidence; `expected_internal_code` independently records the selected internal/recovery result. Never encode `LEDGER_UNAVAILABLE` or `LEDGER_INTEGRITY_UNPROVEN` in the acknowledgement-status field.

Define `Tack = Thandoff + 30s`, where `Thandoff` is the coordinator's trusted monotonic timestamp for durable `HANDOFF_RETURNED`. Define `ack_received_at` as a timestamp assigned by the trusted local acknowledgement ingress only after authenticating and binding the recipient receipt to the exact operation, handoff ID, response digest/count, authority, and event ID. `ack_received_at` and `Tack` MUST use the same protected monotonic clock domain and resolution. A remote authority wall-clock timestamp may be retained in protected audit evidence, but it MUST NOT override the coordinator's local receipt time for deadline classification. If the trusted ingress timestamp or clock-domain relationship cannot be established, treat timing evidence as unavailable; do not compare unnormalized remote and coordinator wall clocks.

The acknowledgement field is selected by the ordered classifier below; the first satisfied rule wins. The classifier is exhaustive only when the authoritative evidence completeness requirements in §2 are met:

1. **`ACK_ACCEPTED`:** authenticated receipt evidence and integrity-verified ledger evidence prove that the acknowledgement and lifecycle `DELIVERED` were atomically committed durably; `ack_received_at < trusted_commit_time < Tack`; and the transaction attempt has a complete authoritative journal record. No other tuple may claim successful delivery.
2. **`ACK_TIMED_OUT` — independently established timeout:** at least one of the following is true: (a) a valid, correctly bound acknowledgement is received at or after `Tack`; (b) the complete authoritative journal records that the one allowed commit attempt was rejected by the strict deadline predicate at or after `Tack`; or (c) the deadline has passed and the configured acknowledgement authority plus complete journal/outcome index positively establish that no valid acknowledgement/DELIVERED commit occurred by `Tack`. A late, authenticated local receipt is enough for this status even if the commit-outcome source later becomes unavailable.
3. **`ACK_TIMED_OUT` — corrupt purported-success ordering:** a purported durable success row records `ack_received_at` that is not strictly earlier than the row's claimed `trusted_commit_time`. Select `ACK_TIMED_OUT` as the fail-closed acknowledgement-field status and separately select `LEDGER_INTEGRITY_UNPROVEN` internally. Do not trust the row as `DELIVERED`; the effective operation is `UNRESOLVED`, the reader is `HALTED`, the reservation remains retained, and no further content may be released or replayed. This use of the acknowledgement token does not certify a valid delivery; it flags an invalid success ordering.
4. **`ACK_COMMIT_FAILED`:** `ack_received_at < Tack`; the complete authoritative evidence proves that the acknowledgement/DELIVERED transaction did not commit; and the one-shot attempt history proves rollback, abort, interruption, or a registered attempt whose execution was interrupted before a commit. A recovery fence and the complete transaction outcome index must establish that no successful commit is still in flight. No further commit attempt is allowed for that receipt.
5. **`WAITING`:** trusted evaluation time is strictly before `Tack`; no durable valid acknowledgement/DELIVERED commit exists; and the complete journal proves that no attempt has rolled back, failed, or been interrupted. A valid receipt MAY already exist if its one allowed acknowledgement commit attempt is still explicitly marked `IN_PROGRESS`. In that pending-attempt case, do not claim `ACK_COMMIT_FAILED` merely because commit has not yet completed.
6. **`ACK_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`:** none of rules 1–5 is provable because the receipt timestamp, acknowledgement authority, journal completeness, or transaction outcome cannot be trusted or retrieved. Keep the acknowledgement field as `ACK_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` even if a separate higher-priority internal ledger result applies; record that result in `expected_internal_code`. A purported success row timestamped at or after `Tack`, without independently established late-receipt evidence or a complete journaled rejection, is not sufficient to invent a timeout: classify its field as `ACK_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` and its internal condition as `LEDGER_INTEGRITY_UNPROVEN`.

Rules 2 and 3 are evaluated before rules 4–6. A contradictory purported success row can never pair an effective `DELIVERED` lifecycle with `ACK_TIMED_OUT` or `ACK_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`: the effective state must be `UNRESOLVED`, reader `HALTED`, with reservation retained and no delivery claim. Existing handling for `ACK_INVALID`, `ACK_MISMATCH`, and `ACK_REPLAYED` remains unchanged.

### 1.2 D1-1a — A receipt with an in-progress commit attempt remains `WAITING`

Amend classifier rule 5. Its required condition is: no durable success and **no rolled-back, failed, aborted, or crash-interrupted attempt**; a receipt may be present while the unique attempt is still in progress. This repairs the pre-deadline gap without accepting two statuses for the same journal state.

Discriminating tests:
- Valid receipt at `Tack−10s`, journal says the unique commit attempt is still `IN_PROGRESS`, authority/journal are readable and complete, status observed at `Tack−2s` → exactly `WAITING`.
- Keep the receipt and all other inputs fixed; change the authoritative attempt outcome to a completed rollback before `Tack` → exactly `ACK_COMMIT_FAILED`, `UNRESOLVED`, reader `HALTED`, reservation retained, no replay.

### 1.3 D1-1b — A contradictory successful row cannot remain `DELIVERED`

If a purported success row records `ack_received_at >= trusted_commit_time`, select `ACK_TIMED_OUT` under rule 3 and internal `LEDGER_INTEGRITY_UNPROVEN`. The invalid success row is not trusted as the effective lifecycle; report `UNRESOLVED`, reader `HALTED`, retained reservation, and no further delivery. Never pair `expected_lifecycle_state=DELIVERED` with `expected_delivery_ack_status=ACK_TIMED_OUT`.

Discriminating test: seed a purported success row with commit timestamp `Tack−2ms` and trusted local receipt timestamp `Tack+1ms`. The validator/recovery oracle must reject `DELIVERED` and `ACK_ACCEPTED`, select `ACK_TIMED_OUT` plus internal `LEDGER_INTEGRITY_UNPROVEN`, and require effective lifecycle `UNRESOLVED`.

### 1.4 D1-1c — A53 has an exact unavailable/integrity tuple

For a purported success row whose `trusted_commit_time` equals `Tack`, when the receipt timestamp is before `Tack` and there is no independently recorded deadline rejection or late receipt, do NOT preserve or invent `ACK_TIMED_OUT`. Use `ACK_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` for the acknowledgement field and `LEDGER_INTEGRITY_UNPROVEN` for the internal result; effective lifecycle and operation record are `UNRESOLVED`, reader `HALTED`, reservation `COMMITTED_RETAINED`, and no delivery claim or replay.

This special case distinguishes a corrupt claimed success at the deadline from a proven, journaled timeout rejection. It closes the previous ambiguity in Entry 288's A53 prose.

## 2. C15-D1-2a and D1-3a/b — Commit linearization, one-shot attempts, and complete recovery evidence

### 2.1 Atomic commit-time predicate

The trusted coordinator MUST enforce `trusted_commit_time < Tack` at the transaction's actual atomic linearization point—the point at which the acknowledgement and `DELIVERED` become one durable committed state. `trusted_commit_time` is assigned by the authoritative ledger for that same linearization point; it is not a timestamp sampled earlier and reused. The strict predicate is evaluated against that ledger-assigned timestamp as part of the atomic commit decision. The coordinator MUST NOT pre-evaluate the predicate at `Tack−ε`, then allow a delayed durable write at or after `Tack`.

Before a commit attempt executes, the authoritative attempt journal entry MUST be durably registered. Each registered attempt has a unique operation-bound `attempt_id`, monotonic journal sequence, and explicit state such as `REGISTERED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMMITTED`, `REJECTED_DEADLINE`, `ROLLED_BACK`, `ABORTED`, or `RECOVERY_ABORTED_NO_COMMIT`. A deadline rejection at or after `Tack` MUST be recorded durably; “may record” is superseded by “MUST record”. The rejection record is not a success row and does not assert delivery.

If a commit attempt's pre-commit work starts before `Tack` but its actual commit linearization would occur at or after `Tack`, abort before writing either success row, record `REJECTED_DEADLINE`, and classify `ACK_TIMED_OUT`. If a success row claims `trusted_commit_time >= Tack`, select `LEDGER_INTEGRITY_UNPROVEN`; never trust the success row as `DELIVERED`.

Discriminating test: allow an early predicate sample at `Tack−1ms`, then force the commit to be delayed until `Tack+2ms`. The earlier sample MUST NOT authorize the commit. The final predicate aborts at the actual linearization point, there is no acknowledgement-success or `DELIVERED` row, the journal records the rejection, and recovery yields `ACK_TIMED_OUT` / `UNRESOLVED`. A second test seeds a success row timestamped exactly `Tack` and expects `LEDGER_INTEGRITY_UNPROVEN`.

### 2.2 D1-3a — No retry after an acknowledgement attempt is failed or interrupted

An accepted trusted receipt consumes one operation-bound acknowledgement-commit opportunity. Once its attempt has been registered, rolled back, rejected, aborted, or interrupted by a crash, **no actor may initiate another acknowledgement/DELIVERED transaction for that receipt**, whether before or after `Tack`. This prohibits both a live-coordinator retry after a pre-deadline rollback and a recovery-generated retry. Recovery may fence an in-flight writer, query the journal/outcome index, append a recovery closure record, and reconcile durable state; it may not retry the commit.

For the same pre-crash durable state, recovery at `Tack−5s` and `Tack+60s` must not initiate any acknowledgement/DELIVERED write and must yield the same acknowledgement status if the same complete evidence is available. For the A50 scenario with a pre-deadline receipt, a registered one-shot attempt interrupted before transaction execution, and proof that no commit occurred, both recoveries produce `ACK_COMMIT_FAILED` / `UNRESOLVED`, retain the reservation, halt new content-bearing operations, and never replay.

Test with a counting writer stub: a pre-deadline rollback followed by a live-coordinator callback and a recovery at `Tack−5s` must cause zero additional commit attempts. Repeat at `Tack+60s`; the resulting tuple is identical.

### 2.3 D1-3b — Registered attempt without a completion record

A durable `REGISTERED` journal entry with no terminal completion does not, by itself, imply source unavailability. If recovery successfully fences the old attempt and the complete authoritative ledger transaction index proves no acknowledgement/DELIVERED commit occurred, recovery closes the attempt as `RECOVERY_ABORTED_NO_COMMIT` and classifies `ACK_COMMIT_FAILED`. If the journal watermark is incomplete, the transaction index cannot be queried, or an old writer cannot be fenced, the outcome is unknown and the status is `ACK_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` (or `LEDGER_INTEGRITY_UNPROVEN` if a supposedly complete history is contradicted by known evidence).

This covers A50 without inferring absence from an empty or unavailable result. The test must change only the evidence completeness: complete journal plus proven no commit → `ACK_COMMIT_FAILED`; a truncated watermark or unfenced writer → `ACK_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`.

### 2.4 Normative definition of complete journal evidence

The “complete journal/watermark” primitive is defined here for schema purposes. The trusted ledger authority must return an authenticated recovery checkpoint for the operation containing:
1. a monotonically increasing journal sequence/watermark and authenticated checkpoint identity;
2. a contiguous, gap-free set of every operation-bound attempt record through that watermark;
3. the transaction outcome-index result for every registered `attempt_id`, including proof of whether a success row became durable;
4. a fencing acknowledgement proving that any prior writer for an interrupted attempt can no longer commit; and
5. a verified ledger/anchor integrity result for the checkpoint and outcome index.

Completeness is accepted only when the checkpoint identity authenticates, the sequence is contiguous, every registered attempt has a terminal result or a recovery closure, the transaction outcome index has been checked, and no writer can still commit. The ledger authority—not requester input or volatile process memory—asserts the signed/authenticated checkpoint and fence. Missing sequence numbers, unavailable authority, a stale/truncated watermark, an unqueried transaction outcome, or inability to fence a writer makes completeness `UNPROVEN`; do not infer “no attempt” or “no commit” from absence. A complete but internally contradictory journal is `LEDGER_INTEGRITY_UNPROVEN`.

Clock-domain rule: the deadline uses the coordinator's monotonic timebase; `ack_received_at` is assigned by that same trusted ingress timebase; `trusted_commit_time` is assigned by the ledger's linearization clock with a protected, documented mapping to the coordinator's monotonic domain. The mapping must have bounded uncertainty strictly smaller than the deadline resolution; if that relation cannot establish whether commit linearized before `Tack`, select `ACK_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`, not a guessed ordering. The actual existence and correctness of these facilities remain `UNPROVEN` until implementation evidence exists.

## 6. C15-D3b-2/3a — Supersede one narrow part of Entry 283 §2.1 for clock-invalidated nonces

Entry 283 §2.1 item 2 currently defines `APPROVAL_LATE` only by arrival at or after the strict challenge deadline. Add this narrow exception without changing the general priority for invalid credentials:

A correctly authenticated and challenge-bound attestation is also classified as `APPROVAL_LATE` when it is received after that exact pending nonce was already invalidated by a recognized clock-safety rule—unknown/unavailable suspend semantics or a detected backward wall-clock step—even if the ordinary challenge deadline has not yet arrived. For this specific case, internal code is `DECISION_EXPIRED`, issuance result is `APPROVAL_LATE`, and Owner-channel result is `OWNER_ATTESTATION_LATE`. The clock-invalidated nonce can never issue a decision or be revived by the attestation.

This exception applies only if the attestation is otherwise authenticated and correctly bound to the same invalidated nonce, with no independent signature, identity, replay, or binding defect. Such independent defects continue to select `OWNER_ATTESTATION_INVALID` / `APPROVAL_CHANNEL_UNTRUSTED` under Entry 283 §2.1 item 1. The new exception supersedes item 2 only for clock-safety invalidation before the nominal challenge deadline; the existing late-challenge case at or after the deadline remains unchanged.

Keep A46 and A47 as distinct fixtures:
- A46: suspend semantics are unknown and invalidate the nonce; a correctly authenticated attestation arrives later but before the nominal challenge deadline; result is `APPROVAL_LATE` / `DECISION_EXPIRED` / `OWNER_ATTESTATION_LATE`.
- A47: a backward wall-clock step invalidates the nonce; a correctly authenticated attestation is subsequently presented using that same nonce; same issuance tuple as A46.
At the fixed visibility endpoint both yield public `REQUEST_UNAVAILABLE` when no eligible decision exists. Before the endpoint, the fixed-window requester response remains `APPROVAL_PENDING`. A no-response control remains `OWNER_NO_RESPONSE` / internal `NONE`; an independently unsigned, replayed, or wrong-nonce attestation remains `OWNER_ATTESTATION_INVALID`.

Discriminating test: for a clock-safety invalidation at `T0+30s` and correctly bound attestation at `T0+40s`, reject `OWNER_ATTESTATION_INVALID` and `APPROVAL_FRESHNESS_EXPIRED`; accept `APPROVAL_LATE`. A nominally late attestation at `T0+121s` with no clock event stays `APPROVAL_LATE` under Entry 283 §2.4. A bad signature or wrong nonce still selects `OWNER_ATTESTATION_INVALID`.
