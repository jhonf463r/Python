# 1. CENTRAL SCIENTIFIC QUESTION

Determine, from the strongest available scientific literature and experimental methodology:

What observable, experimental and falsifiable evidence distinguishes simple accumulation or adaptation from reusable learning, knowledge/belief revision, contextual specialization, relation/knowledge-organization change, causal learning, metacognitive control, self-modeling and self-directed scientific experimentation in artificial systems — and what measurements demonstrate that these changes alter future decisions or behavior rather than merely changing stored records, retrieval, parameters or scores?

The answer must focus on operational distinctions and evidence, not philosophical rhetoric.

---

# 2. CAPABILITY LADDER

Use and critically evaluate this progression:

E0 ACCUMULATION
record/persistence/storage changes.

E1 ADAPTATION
behavior changes under altered inputs/conditions.

E2 REUSABLE LEARNING
information from prior experience improves behavior on later relevant situations beyond simple replay/retrieval.

E3 KNOWLEDGE/BELIEF REVISION
the system changes semantic beliefs/knowledge representations when evidence conflicts, including revision, retraction, uncertainty or supersession.

E4 CONTEXTUAL SPECIALIZATION
the system changes what knowledge/action policy is activated or preferred as a function of context while preserving appropriate generalization.

E5 RELATION / KNOWLEDGE-ORGANIZATION CHANGE
relations, topology, ontology, causal links, abstractions or organization of knowledge change in a meaningful and testable way.

E6 CAUSAL LEARNING
the system learns causal structure or intervention-relevant models, demonstrated with interventions/counterfactuals rather than correlation alone.

E7 METACOGNITIVE CONTROL
the system monitors and uses information about its own uncertainty, prediction quality, errors, limits or strategy selection to alter subsequent behavior.

E8 SELF-MODELING
the system maintains an operational model of relevant aspects of its own state/capabilities/limitations and uses it predictively or causally.

E9 SELF-DIRECTED SCIENTIFIC EXPERIMENTATION
the system autonomously or semi-autonomously generates hypotheses, selects informative experiments, acts, evaluates results and updates subsequent hypotheses/experiments.

E10 VERIFIED FUTURE-DECISION INFLUENCE
verified prior experience produces a persistent representation/relationship/control change that measurably changes later decisions or behavior in new episodes and produces a reproducible downstream consequence.

Do not assume that E0→E10 is empirically established as a universal developmental ladder. Treat it as an analytical framework to compare with the literature and explicitly identify where the literature supports, rejects, merges or complicates these levels.

# 3. REQUIRED SCIENTIFIC DOMAINS

Cover all of the following.

### A. Continual / lifelong learning and plasticity
Investigate:
- continual learning;
- lifelong learning;
- online adaptation;
- stability/plasticity;
- catastrophic forgetting;
- replay/consolidation;
- transfer;
- compositional/generalizable learning;
- mechanisms that distinguish temporary adaptation from durable reusable learning.

### B. Knowledge and belief revision
Investigate:
- AGM belief revision;
- truth-maintenance systems;
- non-monotonic reasoning;
- belief retraction;
- contradiction handling;
- uncertainty-aware revision;
- knowledge-base repair;
- semantic model update.

Identify what actually constitutes a knowledge/belief revision rather than merely adding another record.

### C. Contextualization and knowledge organization
Investigate:
- context-dependent learning;
- contextual memory;
- dynamic knowledge graphs;
- ontology evolution;
- relation learning;
- graph rewiring;
- abstraction/representation change;
- contextual specialization/generalization.

Distinguish:
new node/data
from
new relation
from
topological reorganization
from
functional downstream consequence.

### D. Causal learning
Investigate:
- causal representation learning;
- causal discovery;
- interventions;
- counterfactual evaluation;
- active causal learning;
- experimental design for causal inference.

Prioritize evidence where interventions discriminate causal understanding from correlation or pattern matching.

### E. Metacognition and self-monitoring
Investigate:
- confidence/uncertainty calibration;
- error monitoring;
- prediction monitoring;
- metacognitive sensitivity;
- strategy selection based on self-estimated competence;
- self-models;
- calibration and selective prediction;
- behavior that demonstrates metacognitive control rather than verbal self-description.

### F. Autonomous scientific discovery / experimentation
Investigate systems that:
- generate hypotheses;
- identify informative experiments;
- select experiments;
- execute or orchestrate experiments;
- interpret results;
- update beliefs/hypotheses;
- choose subsequent experiments based on prior evidence.

Distinguish:
automated pipeline
from
closed-loop adaptive experimentation
from
genuinely self-directed scientific search.

### G. Machine consciousness indicators
Treat this as a downstream and separate research layer.

Investigate:
- theory-derived indicators;
- computationally testable consciousness indicators;
- theory-neutral or adversarial evaluation;
- global availability/integration;
- self-model-related indicators;
- limits of behavioral self-report;
- false-positive risks.

Do not claim that any current system is conscious unless the evidence directly supports that claim. “Superconsciousness” must remain an operational hypothesis, not an established fact.

# 4. SCIENTIFIC DELTA MODEL

Use this conceptual delta decomposition:

ΔW = parameter/weight change
ΔM = memory/storage change
ΔK = semantic knowledge/belief change
ΔR = relation/topology/organization change
ΔC = context/conditioning change
ΔD = future decision-policy/action selection change
ΔB = behavior change
ΔO = downstream outcome change

For each scientific domain, explain which deltas are actually measured in the literature and which are commonly conflated.

The critical discrimination is:

ΔM alone ≠ learning
ΔW alone ≠ learning
retrieval improvement alone ≠ knowledge revision
ΔK alone ≠ relation reorganization
ΔR alone ≠ future decision influence
ΔD alone ≠ causal improvement
ΔB alone ≠ understanding
self-report alone ≠ metacognition
automated experiment execution alone ≠ self-directed experimentation.

---

# 5. FALSE-POSITIVE CONTROLS

Explicitly identify how experiments can produce false impressions of higher capability.

At minimum consider:

- memorization/retrieval mistaken for learning;
- prompt/context leakage;
- benchmark contamination;
- parameter tuning mistaken for semantic learning;
- repeated exposure mistaken for generalization;
- score changes without useful behavior changes;
- graph growth mistaken for relation reorganization;
- correlation mistaken for causal learning;
- verbal confidence mistaken for metacognition;
- scripted tool use mistaken for self-directed experimentation;
- human-designed experiment selection mistaken for autonomous scientific reasoning;
- persistence mistaken for future causal influence.

For every strong claim, state what control or ablation is necessary to rule out the simpler explanation.

# 6. EXPERIMENTAL METHODOLOGY

Prioritize scientific designs with:

- before/after state capture;
- held-out or novel episodes;
- controlled interventions;
- ablations;
- counterfactual tests where appropriate;
- longitudinal evaluation;
- transfer/generalization tests;
- independent replication;
- preregistration where available/relevant;
- explicit provenance of measurements;
- statistically defensible comparisons;
- separation of training data from evaluation data;
- measurement of downstream decisions and outcomes.

Prefer experiments capable of answering:

Did verified experience change a representation, relationship, policy or decision in a way that persists, generalizes and produces a measurable downstream effect?
