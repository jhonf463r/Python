## 1. Research questions raised

The conversation pursued three related but distinct fundamental questions:

1. Whether a universal cognitive algorithm exists across biological and artificial intelligence systems.
2. Whether a universal lifecycle exists for the birth, maturation, certification, integration, autonomous use, metacognition, replacement, and retirement of an artificial cognitive capability.
3. Whether a universal, minimal and irreducible `Estado Cognitivo Cero (EC0)` must precede the emergence of any cognitive capability.

The user explicitly required adversarial evaluation: do not assume universality, attempt to destroy both the universal and non-universal hypotheses, and distinguish evidence from inference.

**STATUS:** HISTORICAL RESEARCH OBJECTIVE.

## 2. Scope preserved

The requested interdisciplinary comparison included neuroscience, developmental psychology, cognitive science, artificial intelligence, continual learning, curriculum learning, developmental robotics, active inference, predictive processing, free-energy approaches, systems theory, control theory, information theory, evolutionary biology, epistemology, mathematics, category theory, metacognition, and autonomous learning.

No implementation or architecture was requested as the scientific output.

**STATUS:** HISTORICAL RESEARCH SCOPE.

## 3. Partial research result that was actually returned

A prior research report in the conversation argued that current evidence did not support a single complete universal theory of cognitive capabilities. It described partial convergence around adaptive learning, feedback, predictive/error-based processes, and hierarchical or modular organization, while emphasizing that concrete mechanisms and developmental sequences vary by organism, architecture, and environment.

The report therefore treated a completely universal theory as **not demonstrated** and a partially universal framework as more plausible.

This result is preserved only as **historical research output**, not as a canonical IABV scientific conclusion, because the retrieved research package used mixed-quality sources and did not establish universal necessity across all substrates.

## 4. Universal lifecycle question

The conversation proposed testing whether stages such as emergence, activation, acquisition, stabilization, certification, integration, autonomous use, metacognition, and replacement are universal.

The historical research trajectory found no evidence in the available material establishing that every capability must pass through one fixed ordered sequence. The strongest recurring pattern was instead a set of possible developmental constraints whose exact realization may depend on substrate, architecture, task, and environment.

**STATUS:** PARTIAL / NOT A CLOSED UNIVERSAL LAW.

## 5. EC0 question

The strongest hypothesis under evaluation was:

`H1: a universal, unique, irreducible EC0 precedes all cognition.`

The adversarial alternative was:

`H0: no universal EC0 exists; initial cognitive conditions differ by organism, architecture, substrate, algorithm, and environment.`

The conversation explicitly required the investigation to search for counterexamples, remove candidate properties one by one, and distinguish physical, informational, causal, computational, and philosophical prerequisites.

No final scientific verdict on EC0 was successfully returned in the available research output before the conversation moved into archaeology/preservation work.

**STATUS:** OPEN RESEARCH QUESTION.

## 6. Epistemic guardrail for these research threads

The following must remain explicit:

- research prompt != scientific result;
- model-generated synthesis != empirical proof;
- analogy across biological and artificial systems != substrate-independent law;
- recurring pattern != universal invariant;
- mathematical expressibility != empirical necessity;
- absence of counterexample in the searched literature != proof of universality.

These guardrails are consistent with the project's broader evidence-first methodology already preserved in the main conversation archaeology record.

## 7. Relationship to IABV

These research questions were intentionally kept separate from IABV implementation. They may inform future scientific justification, but no architectural change, production claim, or implementation decision should be inferred from them automatically.

The project should retain the distinction between:

`SCIENTIFIC HYPOTHESIS`
`→`
`EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE`
`→`
`CROSS-SUBSTRATE REPLICATION`
`→`
`PROJECT PRINCIPLE`

Only the latter may become a project-level invariant after adequate evidence.
